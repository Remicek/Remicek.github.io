HowYouDoin privacy policy

Last updated: 11 November 2026

HowYouDoin is a free, open-source chat app that brings your WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, Telegram, Signal, Slack, Bluesky, LinkedIn, Discord, X and Matrix conversations into one inbox. This policy explains what happens to your data.

What we collect

None of your messages, contacts or logins. Your messages never pass through HowYouDoin's servers, and there are no accounts with us, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The developers never receive your messages, contacts, login details or usage data. The app contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting libraries from other companies either.

The only server of ours is the call service, used for the optional calls through a link. It keeps how many call minutes each installation has used, and what was bought with Premium; see "Call minutes and Premium" below.

What stays on your phone

To show your chats, the app keeps on your phone:

  • the login sessions of the networks you connect (session cookies, device keys, encryption keys), in the app's private storage, encrypted with a key from Android's Keystore (WhatsApp's, Signal's and Messenger's encrypted-chat databases included);
  • your chats, messages, contacts' names and pictures, and downloaded photos and files;
  • your settings, including the app lock PIN as a salted one-way hash (not the PIN itself);
  • your own Telegram API keys, if you enter them (Settings → Telegram API keys), encrypted like the logins.

Your chats and messages are never copied to the cloud. Settings → Back up logins is on unless you turn it off: it puts only the network logins in your phone's Google backup, and only when that backup is end-to-end encrypted with your screen lock, so a new phone can reconnect without logging in again. With it off, nothing is backed up. Disconnecting a network in the app deletes its data from the phone; uninstalling the app or clearing its data deletes everything.

Where your data goes

When you connect a network, the app talks directly to that network's servers (Meta for WhatsApp, Messenger and Instagram, Telegram, Signal, Slack, Bluesky, LinkedIn, Discord, X, and the Matrix homeserver of your account) to send and receive your messages, just as the official apps do. Those companies' own privacy policies apply to what they receive.

  • Messenger, Instagram, Slack, LinkedIn, Discord and X are logged into on their own web pages, shown inside the app; those pages are theirs, and the app only keeps the session they create.
  • Bluesky is signed into with your handle and an app password you create in Bluesky; the app keeps the app password on your phone to sign in again.
  • X's messages (X Chat) are end-to-end encrypted. Your X Chat passcode unlocks your keys from X's key backup once; the keys and the passcode then stay on your phone, encrypted.
  • Profile pictures, photos and files are downloaded from those networks' servers.
  • Voice and video calls (Matrix) connect to the other person directly or through the relay server of your Matrix homeserver.

Calls through a link (optional)

The other networks' own calls can't be made or answered in HowYouDoin. Instead, you can start a separate call and send its link in a chat; the other person joins it in their browser. Only when you start one:

  • The app asks HowYouDoin's call service for a call, and the call itself is connected through Cloudflare's servers (the call service runs on Cloudflare too). They see your IP address, as any server you connect to does, and the call's random name.
  • The call is end-to-end encrypted with a key that only goes in the link you send, after the #, a part browsers never send to a server. Neither the call service nor Cloudflare can see or hear the call, and neither stores it.
  • The link is sent as an ordinary message in that chat, so the network carries it like any other message.
  • A call has room for two people. It closes 15 minutes after it's created if nobody joins, when either of you hangs up, and after at most 4 hours. Once it closes, the call service keeps nothing about the call itself, only the minutes it used (below).

Call minutes and Premium

Calls through a link come with free minutes every month, and HowYouDoin Premium (a Google Play subscription) or a video top-up adds more. To count them, the call service keeps:

  • a random ID the app makes when it's installed (not linked to your name, phone number, Google account or any network account), sent with each call you start;
  • for that ID, or for your Premium subscription: the month, the video and audio minutes used in it, and any bought video time left.

When you buy Premium or a top-up, Google Play handles the payment; we never see your card or Google account. The app sends the purchase token Google Play gives it to the call service, which asks Google Play whether the purchase is paid. The service then keeps the subscription's Google Play order number and when it is paid until, and a one-way hash of each top-up's purchase token (so one purchase can't be used twice). Google Play's own privacy policy applies to the purchase.

This is kept while it can still be used: the minutes of the current month, and bought time until it runs out. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data gives it a new random ID; what was kept for the old one is then no longer connected to your phone. Premium and bought time come back through Settings → Premium → Restore purchases.

Server mode (optional)

If you choose to connect through a Matrix homeserver, your messages go through that server and the bridges running there. Whoever operates that server (you, or someone you trust) is responsible for that data.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, a report (the error, the app version and your phone model, not your messages) is saved on your phone. It's never sent automatically. You can read, share or delete it under Settings → Crash reports, and choose whether the app offers to share it after a crash.

Permissions

  • Notifications: to tell you about new messages.
  • Internet: to connect to the networks.
  • Running in the background / starting at boot: so messages keep arriving when the app is closed.
  • Fingerprint / face (optional): to unlock the app; Android handles the biometric check, the app never sees your fingerprint.
  • Microphone (optional): only while you record a voice message or are in a call; the recording is sent to the chat you recorded it in.
  • Camera (optional): only during a video call.
  • Full-screen notifications: to show an incoming call on a locked phone.
  • Photos and files you pick: only the ones you choose to send.

Children

HowYouDoin is not directed at children under the minimum age the connected networks require.

Terms of use

Using the app is also subject to the terms of use, which explain that the app is unofficial, provided "as is", and that networks may block accounts that use it.

Changes and contact

Changes to this policy are published with the app's source code. Questions: open an issue in the project's repository.
